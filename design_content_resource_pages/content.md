## H2 — Moving Advice and Tips (tabbed: Choose a Mover / Avoid Scams / Save Money)

### Tab: Choose a mover
- **Check the USDOT number** — a licensed mover follows federal moving rules and gives you more legal protection.
- **Read recent reviews** — recent reviews show how a company handles problems now, not just its overall rating.
- **Compare at least 3 quotes** — on the same inventory list, so you're comparing the same move.
- **Ask what's included** — packing, storage, and disassembly are bundled by some movers, billed separately by others.

### Tab: Avoid scams
- **Beware unusually low quotes** — scam movers quote low to win the booking, then raise the price after loading.
- **Never pay a large deposit** — legitimate movers rarely require large upfront payment.
- **Get everything in writing** — estimate, delivery window, and fees should be in the contract before moving day.
- **Check FMCSA's Protect Your Move** — search the mover's name for unresolved complaints before booking.

### Tab: Save money
- **Book 4–8 weeks ahead** — last-minute bookings cost more because fewer trucks/dates are available.
- **Move in the off-season** — rates are typically lower outside May–September peak season.
- **Pack smaller items yourself** — professional packing adds labor and material costs.
- **Declutter before you quote** — cost is tied to weight and volume, so donating items lowers price directly.

## H2 — Frequently Asked Questions (FAQPage schema)

### How far in advance should I book long-distance movers?
4–8 weeks before your move date is the safe window, and earlier if you're moving between May and September.

### How do I know if a moving company is legitimate?
Check their USDOT number on the FMCSA database, read recent (not just overall) reviews, and get your estimate in writing before booking.

### What's the cheapest way to move long-distance?
Renting a truck and doing the labor yourself is typically cheapest. A moving container is a middle ground — you load it, the company drives.

### What is considered a long-distance move?
Most movers define it as a move that crosses state lines, or covers more than 100 miles, regardless of whether it stays in-state.

### Can moving companies raise the price after loading my items?
Only with a non-binding estimate. A binding or binding-not-to-exceed estimate protects you from this — always ask which type you're getting.

### How much should I tip long-distance movers?
Tipping is optional but common for good service — a typical guideline is $20–$50 per mover for a half-day move, or $40–$100 per mover for a full day.

### Do I need separate moving insurance?
Movers are required to offer released value protection (about $0.60 per pound per item) or full value protection. Third-party moving insurance is a separate policy you can buy on top of that.

### Do long-distance movers ship cars too?
Some do, but it's not standard across the industry — many movers partner with a separate auto-transport company instead. Ask directly before assuming it's included.

### What's the difference between a moving broker and a carrier?
A carrier owns the trucks and does the move itself. A broker arranges the move by connecting you with a carrier. Always confirm which one you're booking and get the carrier's own USDOT number.

### How long does an interstate move take?
Anywhere from a few days to a few weeks, depending on distance and season. Cross-country moves often land in the 10–20 day range, especially during peak months.

### Is it cheaper to move on a weekday?
Yes — Monday through Thursday moves are typically cheaper than weekends, and pricing also spikes at the start and end of each month.
