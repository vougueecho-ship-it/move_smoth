Moving advice and tips

Pick a topic below.

Check the USDOT number

A licensed mover follows federal moving rules and gives you more legal protection during an interstate move.

Read recent reviews

Recent reviews show how a company handles problems now — not just its overall historical rating.

Compare at least 3 quotes

Multiple quotes on the same inventory list show you what a fair price actually looks like.

Ask what's included

Packing, storage, and disassembly are included by some movers and billed separately by others.

Beware unusually low quotes

Scam movers often quote low to win the booking, then raise the price after your items are loaded.

Never pay a large deposit

Most legitimate movers don't require a large upfront payment before your move date.

Get everything in writing

Your estimate, delivery window, and any extra fees should be in the contract before moving day.

Check FMCSA's Protect Your Move

Search the mover's name in the federal database for unresolved complaints before booking.

Book 4–8 weeks ahead

Last-minute bookings are pricier because fewer dates and trucks are available.

Move in the off-season

Rates are typically lower outside the May–September peak moving season.

Pack smaller items yourself

Professional packing adds labor and material costs — do the easy boxes yourself.

Declutter before you quote

Cost is tied to weight and volume — selling or donating items lowers your price directly.

Key takeaways

  • There's no universal "best" mover — the right pick depends on your home size, route, and timeline.
  • Always compare at least 3 quotes and confirm whether each is binding or non-binding before you sign.
  • Verify the USDOT number on the FMCSA site before booking any interstate mover.
  • Book 4–8 weeks ahead — earlier during the May–September peak season.
